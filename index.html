Official Release Status & Rockstar Cycles

19 November 2026

Grand Theft Auto VII is not announced, not dated, and not out. The only numbered GTA in stores this week is VI, which launched today — 19 November 2026 — on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. That is the entire official status report.

Rockstar’s modern gaps are getting longer, not shorter. The studio that once shipped a GTA almost every year now treats each numbered entry as a platform:

  • GTA V — 17 September 2013. Then a decade of GTA Online.
  • Red Dead Redemption 2 — 26 October 2018. About five years and one month after V.
  • GTA VI — 19 November 2026. About eight years after RDR2, thirteen years after V.

The V → RDR2 gap was already a generation. The RDR2 → VI gap ate a console cycle, a pandemic, a delayed 2025 window, a May 2026 target, and finally this November date. VI will now do what V did: absorb live-service years, expansions, and a later PC port. None of that is a GTA 7 teaser. It is the opposite — a reason VII waits.

A realistic VII window

If Rockstar repeats the ~8-year GTA-to-GTA gap, you land around 2034. If VI Online becomes another decade-long economy, slide the window to 2034–2036 and stop pretending 2029 is in play. The 3D-era annual pace is dead. So is the idea that a sequel gets announced at launch. Rockstar does not cannibalize a new SKU in week one.

What would actually move this article: a Take-Two earnings mention of a successor; a rated listing; a hiring spike that is not VI support. Until then the answer stays one syllable, and the clock above keeps running.

Take-Two Patents & Next-Gen Tech

19 November 2026

Patents are not trailers. They are legal descriptions of systems Take-Two Interactive wanted to protect. Several granted filings from the VI development years are public at the USPTO. They describe how a future RAGE title could run denser traffic and interiors — including, someday, a VII. They do not name GTA 7.

US11684855B2 — virtual navigation

Granted 27 June 2023. Inventors include Simon Parr and David Hynd; assigned to Take-Two. Title: “System and method for virtual navigation in a gaming environment.” The claim is a coarse graph built from a low-level road-network graph. NPCs plan a route on the coarse graph, then expand it back into a full low-level path.

Why it matters: classic GTA traffic is local — the car in front of you, the lane beside you, last frame’s collision. A hierarchical graph lets pedestrians and vehicles account for traffic, weather, blocked roads, exits, and police paths without simulating the entire city at full resolution. That is how you fake a living map on a console budget. VI almost certainly drinks from this well. VII would inherit it, not invent it.

US10987587B2 — objects and environments

Granted 27 April 2021. Inventors include Aaron Garbut, Klaas Schilstra, Flavius Alecu, and John Whyte. The system stores metadata-tagged assets (identifiers plus property tags) and a ruleset that can pull those assets at runtime. The patent’s own example is procedural interiors: ask for a worn-down room, or a theater crowd in casual dress, and the engine composes it from interchangeable tagged parts instead of hand-placing every sofa.

Fan blogs turned this into “70% of buildings are enterable.” That percentage is not in the patent. The patent is real. The percentage is folklore. Keep them apart.

RAGE, and what it is not

Rockstar’s engine remains RAGE — Rockstar Advanced Game Engine. Public filings around runtime animation retargeting and inverse kinematics (including application US20250095261A1, published 2025) show continued work on characters that meet stairs and slopes without canned clips. “RAGE 9” is community shorthand, not an official product name. Treat version numbers on YouTube as decoration.

Bottom line for this wiki: Take-Two patented smarter NPC routing and tagged procedural sets. Those tools outlive VI. They are not a GTA 7 announcement. If a later filing names a successor project, it will be cited here with a number, a date, and a caveat.

Setting & Map Speculation

19 November 2026

GTA VI went back to Vice City — the Florida stand-in now framed as Leonida. That choice closes one long request and opens the only interesting map question left: where does VII even go after a whole state?

The most cited historical remark is from Leslie Benzies, then head of Rockstar North, in a December 2012 Digital Trends interview for Vice City’s tenth anniversary. Asked about returning to Miami, he said a return was always possible, then sketched a larger ambition:

“Of course at some point we would like to have one big world containing all our cities and let the player fly between them and revisit their favorite areas, and in that context re-imagining Vice City would be very interesting.” — Leslie Benzies, Digital Trends, 2012

That is not a leak. It is a 14-year-old wish from a producer who later left the company. It still frames how fans talk: Liberty City, Vice City, San Andreas, and the older 3D cities as terminals in one flight network. Hardware in 2012 could not hold it. Hardware in 2026 can hold a state. Hardware in 2035 is the actual variable.

Other documented threads

  • Era vs. place. In the same interview Benzies noted that Vice City was as much the 1980s as Miami, and that revisiting the city in another decade would “feel strange.” VI tested that by returning to Florida in the present. VII could treat Vice as settled and leave.
  • International pitches. Benzies said almost every city on Earth was pitched in early Vice City meetings, including a revisit of London (GTA London 1969 / 1961 already existed as expansions). London remains the respectable international rumor because it has franchise precedent, not because a map file leaked.
  • Liberty City (HD). The most requested “next” after a Vice State is a full modern Liberty. It is also the most obvious. Rockstar tends to avoid obvious until it is useful.

This page will log named sources, not Discord screenshots. If VII is a multi-city flight, Benzies called the fantasy in 2012. If it is one new city, that is the entire history of the series. Either way, nobody at Rockstar has to tell us this decade.
